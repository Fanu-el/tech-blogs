# Writing Readable Tech Content

Technical writing is a skill. Learn how to make your articles clear, scannable, and useful.

## The Reader's Perspective

Most people don't read articles—they scan them. They're looking for the answer to their problem.

- Use clear headings that match search queries
- Keep paragraphs short (2-3 sentences)
- Lead with the most important idea
- Use examples to clarify concepts

## Structure That Works

A strong tech article has a predictable structure:

1. **Problem statement**: What problem does this solve?
2. **Why it matters**: When would someone care?
3. **The solution**: Clear steps or explanation
4. **Real examples**: Code, diagrams, or scenarios
5. **Key takeaways**: What should readers remember?

## Writing Tips

**Be specific**: "Use arrow functions" is better than "Modern JavaScript is useful."

**Show, don't tell**: Include code examples, screenshots, or diagrams.

**Think of objections**: Anticipate questions your reader might ask and address them.

## Technical Accuracy

Get the details right. If your explanation is incorrect, readers will lose trust.

- Test code examples before publishing
- Link to authoritative sources
- Update posts when information changes

## Publishing Cadence

Consistency matters more than frequency. Regular, high-quality posts build an audience.

Aim for a sustainable rhythm—whether that's weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly.
